# Dominion API balance report

Generated by `node --import tsx sim/dominion/run.ts --seasons 200 --sweep 100`. 200 seasons of 168 ticks, four bots (one per strategy), 10 maps of 40-80 worlds, random start rings, and a late starter in 79 seasons. Everything is deterministic: the same command gives the same report.

## The two questions

### 1. Does one strategy always win?

**No.** The most successful strategy, the terraformer, wins 35% of 200 seasons (even would be 25%); every strategy wins some seasons.

Winner spread (normalized entropy, 1.00 = all four equally often): 0.95. The top strategy differs between maps: 3 different strategies are the most frequent winner on at least one of the 10 maps.

### 2. Does the early leader always stay ahead?

**No.** The leader at 25% of the season finishes first in 34% of seasons (the leader at 50% in 40%). With four players a random leader would hold on 25% of the time.

### And can a late starter at the edge still finish well?

**Not at the defaults.** Late starters (79 seasons; they join 20-30% into the season at the edge) finish with a mean rank of 2.89 of 4, in the top two 37% of the time, and win 8 times.

## Dominance and the fix

No strategy wins more than 40% of seasons at the defaults (top: terraformer at 35%), so no fix run was needed.

## Reading the results

- With `bandwidthExponent` = 0.7 the scorcher wins 79% and the leader at 25% wins 80%: without the square-root curve, the biggest empire simply out-acts everyone. The sqrt rule is doing real work.
- With `bandwidthExponent` = 1 the scorcher wins 81% and the leader at 25% wins 82%: without the square-root curve, the biggest empire simply out-acts everyone. The sqrt rule is doing real work.
- The newcomer floor and the cheap edge are not enough for a late starter: mean rank 2.89 of 4. A catch-up grant helps (`lateJoinGrant` = 60 gives a mean rank of 2.23 in the sweep) without changing which strategies win.

## Caveats

- The bots are simple first-week scripts. A strategy that loses here may only be badly played; a strategy that wins may be exploiting a bot that does not defend well. Treat the numbers as a first read on the rules, not a verdict on human play.
- Every season has exactly one player per strategy, so "win rate" means "wins against one of each other strategy".
- The score is resources in the bank at the end of the season. A different victory rule (held worlds, a mix) would change which strategy is strongest.
