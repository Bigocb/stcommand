stcommand Player Guide

Your fleet trades on its own between your orders. This guide shows you how to read it, steer it, and avoid the expensive mistakes.

Quick start

Do these five things in your first session. Nothing here spends credits.

Step 1

Look at Home

Open Tower's Home tab. Note the wallet, the fleet mode, and the activity list. That is the whole fleet in one screen.

Step 2

Check the fleet mode

Home has an AUTO / HALT switch. AUTO lets the fleet trade. HALT stops every ship from starting new work. Leave it on AUTO unless you have a reason.

Step 3

Open one ship

Go to Fleet and tap any ship. The sheet shows its role, where it is, its cargo and its fuel.

Step 4

Read the metrics

Open Metrics. Use the 1h and Since reset buttons to compare short and long windows.

Step 5

Ask the co-pilot

Open Co-pilot and ask, "What is the fleet doing right now?" It answers from live data. It can change nothing until you confirm.

Before you change anything Read first, act second. Read the fleet status and the last hour of metrics, then decide. Most problems that look urgent settle themselves within a few hours.

How the game works

SpaceTraders is a trading game. Ships buy goods at one market, carry them, and sell them at another market where the price is higher. The difference, after fuel and jump fees, is profit.

  • The universe resets every week. Everything starts again: markets, routes, gates, and your starting credits. Your score for a week is the credits you end with, plus the value of cargo still in your ships.
  • Prices move with stock. Buying a good pushes its price up at that market, and selling pushes it down. Prices recover slowly, a few percent an hour. Spreading trades over several markets keeps profit up.
  • Gates link systems. A jump gate lets a ship move to a neighbouring system. A gate works only when its construction is finished at both ends. Each jump costs about 5,000 credits and a cooldown.
  • Fuel is taken up front. When a flight starts, the game takes its full fuel cost. A ship on a long leg shows the same fuel until it arrives.
  • The fleet runs itself. Ships keep working between your orders. That is the goal, so act less than you think you need to.

Tower: the phone screens

Tower is built for a phone. Its tabs run along the bottom of the screen.

Phone shows old screens? Tower's script can be cached for up to four hours. If a button does nothing or a screen looks out of date, reload the page. If it is still stale, ask the operator to clear the cache.

Changing a doctrine value in Tower

Doctrine is the fleet's standing rules. In Tower, open More, then Rules. Each rule has an on/off switch. A rule with a number also has a value box. Type a new number and leave the box: it saves at once. A value outside the allowed range is refused and the old value comes back.

Deck: the desktop screens

Deck is the desktop version of the same information. Use it when you want more detail on one screen: ship lists, route boards, doctrine and operations panels. Anything you can change in Tower, you can change in Deck, and both show the same live state.

The co-pilot

The co-pilot is a chat assistant. It can:

  • Answer questions about the fleet, the markets, the ledger, the metrics and the gates, using live data.
  • Propose a change to a ship or to the whole fleet.

It cannot run a change on its own. Every change is a proposal until you confirm it.

Example

Holding a ship

You type: "Hold THEO-17 at its market."

The co-pilot replies with a proposal and an id, for example a1b2c3, and a one-line summary of what would happen.

To run it, reply /confirm a1b2c3. To drop it, reply /cancel a1b2c3. A proposal expires after 30 minutes.

Proposals the co-pilot can make today: set a ship's role, hold a ship, release a ship, halt the whole fleet, and resume the whole fleet. More kinds are planned; see docs/copilot-phase-2-plan.md.

Settings. The gear icon on the co-pilot screen holds the language model settings: the service address, the model name, and the key. The key is stored only on the server. Never paste it into the chat.

Ship roles

Every ship has one role. Changing a role interrupts whatever the ship is doing, so change it only between jobs.

RoleWhat it doesLeave it alone when
traderBuys and sells on its own, round trip, using the best route it can reach.Almost always. The dispatcher picks its routes.
miner / siphonerExtracts ore or gas for traders and the warehouse.It is feeding a trader or a warehouse.
surveyorScouts mining sites so miners work faster. Carries no cargo.It is surveying.
explorerJumps to unvisited systems to chart them. Costs jump fees.It is mid-jump.
tourVisits every market in a system to keep prices fresh.It is partway through a system.
keeperSits at one market so its prices never go stale.Almost always. Keepers stay where they are.

Moving ships

There are three ways to move a ship. Pick by distance.

  • Dispatch sends a ship to a waypoint in its current system. It stops there.
  • Jump sends a ship through one gate to a neighbouring system.
  • Tour sends a ship to a system more than one jump away. It finds the gates itself. Give it a system name, not a waypoint.
Jump planner On a ship's sheet, the planner lists the systems it can jump to. A row greyed out means that gate is not finished yet, or the ship is moving. Greyed rows cannot be selected.

Reading the metrics

Metrics answers one question: is the fleet making money, and where? It has four numbers you will watch.

  • Net trading is profit from completed sales, less fuel and jump fees. It is the honest figure for how the trading is going.
  • Wallet change is the real change in credits. It also includes purchases and cargo still held, so it can differ from net trading for a while.
  • Net per hour is net trading divided by the hours in the window. Compare windows of the same length.
  • Since reset covers everything since the current universe began. Use it for the week's score.

Example

A bad hour

The 1-hour chart shows a large red bar. Before you act, check three things: did a ship buy a lot of cargo that is still unsold (the wallet dips but net stays positive); did a sale go below cost on purpose; did jumps cost more than usual. A single bad hour inside a good week is normal.

Rolling windows mix weeks A 3-day or 1-hour window can include trades from the old universe straight after a reset. For a fair picture of this week, use Since reset.

Supply chains

Most goods are made from other goods. A market that exports a good needs inputs it imports, and those inputs may need inputs of their own. The chain runs from raw ore to the finished good.

Made goodMade fromMade from (one step further back)
FAB_MATSIRON, QUARTZ_SANDIRON comes from IRON_ORE
ADVANCED_CIRCUITRYELECTRONICS, MICROPROCESSORS(check the chain health view for the next step)

Two words describe every market on a chain:

  • Supply is how much of a good a market holds: SCARCE, LIMITED, MODERATE, HIGH or ABUNDANT.
  • Activity is how strongly it produces (for an export) or consumes (for an import): RESTRICTED, WEAK, GROWING or STRONG.
The rule of thumb A producer stays RESTRICTED while any of its inputs is SCARCE. Aim to hold every input at HIGH. Aiming for ABUNDANT costs more and makes the market consume more, so it needs more input to stay supplied.

Feeds are how you supply an input. A feed is a crew that sources a good, by mining or buying, and sells it into a target market. A feed can stop itself once the target reaches a supply level, so you can set it to HIGH and let it stop.

Circuits

A ship that sells its cargo and drives back empty earns nothing on the way back. A circuit fixes that. It is a pair of trips planned together:

  1. Leg 1: buy good A at market X, carry it to market Y and sell it.
  2. Leg 2: from Y, buy good B, which is cheap near Y, and carry it back toward X, then sell it.

The ship is loaded on both trips. The planner only plans leg 2 when it can start near where leg 1 ends, within the circuit reach (10 minutes of flying by default). Leg 2 is kept for that ship until leg 1's cargo is sold. If leg 2's margin falls to half of what was planned, or the market leaves the board, the planner drops it and the ship takes new work.

Example (illustrative numbers)

A two-leg circuit

Leg 1 buys CLOTHING at market A for 3,700 a unit and sells it at market B for 4,500. Leg 2 buys COPPER at market B and sells it back near A. The ship earns on the way out and again on the way back, instead of flying the return empty. Check the trader's sheet to see its circuit: the explanation line names both legs.

Two rules control circuits: Two-leg circuits (on, with a 75 % weight) and Circuits across a gate (on, for a circuit that crosses a jump gate). See the doctrine reference.

Building a gate

A gate joins two systems. It works only when its construction is finished at both ends, and construction takes materials delivered to the gate's market. Each gate has its own list. In one gate we checked in October, the list was:

MaterialNeeded
FAB_MATS1,600
ADVANCED_CIRCUITRY400
QUANTUM_STABILIZERS1

Those numbers are an example. Read your own gate's list from the gate status view before you plan anything.

  1. Read the gate. Ask the co-pilot for the gate status of the system. Note which gate is unfinished and what it needs.
  2. Read the chain. Open chain health. For each material it lists the producer market, each input, the cheapest source, and the weakest input. The weakest input is the one to feed first.
  3. Feed the inputs. Start feeds for the weakest inputs, with the stop level set to HIGH.
  4. Protect the chain. The Protect the gate's supply chain rule keeps the inputs out of ordinary trading while the gate is unfinished. Feeds and deliberate moves still work.
  5. Buy the finished materials when the market is ready. A producer that is RESTRICTED makes little. Wait until its activity rises before buying, or the price climbs as you buy.
Refill is slow One market recovered only a few units an hour during the October build. A gate needing a thousand-plus units cannot be bought faster than the market makes them. Feed the inputs early, from the first hour.

Walkthrough: a first game

This walkthrough follows one week from reset to a first gate. Each step says what you should see, what to do, and what to avoid. The numbers are illustrative.

Step 1 of 9 · Hour 0

Look before you buy

You see: a small wallet, a few ships, one home system. Do: read Home and the fleet list. Find the home system and the markets nearby. Avoid: buying anything yet.

Step 2 of 9 · Hour 0 to 1

Send the command ship to price the markets

You see: the command ship starts as a tour ship. The route list is empty, because no market has been priced yet. Do: send the command ship on a tour of the home system with dispatch_tour, and let it visit every market. Each visit records the prices. Avoid: starting traders before the home markets are priced, since they have no routes to take. Wait until the route list shows routes.

Step 3 of 9 · Hours 1 to 2

Start the traders on the nearest good routes

You see: the route list. Several goods show a positive profit per trip. Do: leave the traders on AUTO and let the dispatcher assign them. Avoid: moving traders by hand.

Step 4 of 9 · Hours 2 to 3

Feed the gate's weakest input

You see: the gate status and chain health for your system. One input is LIMITED or SCARCE. Do: start a feed for it and set the stop level to HIGH. Avoid: setting it to ABUNDANT.

Step 5 of 9 · Day 1

Let the markets breathe

You see: profit per trip falling as you sell into the same markets. Do: check the metrics once in the morning and once in the evening. Let the sales spread across markets. Avoid: four sales in a row at one market.

Step 6 of 9 · Day 1, afternoon

Watch a circuit happen

You see: a trader's sheet names two legs. Do: nothing. The planner drops leg 2 on its own if its margin halves. Avoid: overriding the circuit by hand.

Step 7 of 9 · Day 2

Widen to a second system

You see: the home markets start to look saturated and the route list shrinks. Do: find a system with many markets, within one or two gate jumps of home, and send a tour ship there first. Avoid: sending traders across the galaxy before their first sale covers the jump cost.

Step 8 of 9 · Days 2 to 5

Deliver the gate's materials

You see: chain health shows the producer market's ask price and its supply. Do: buy the finished materials in steps when the producer's activity rises, and watch the price. Avoid: a tight buying loop. Price rises with each lot.

Step 9 of 9 · End of week

Score the week

You see: Metrics, Since reset. Do: read net trading and net per hour, and check the wallet change against cargo still held. Note what to change next week. Avoid: drawing conclusions from a rolling 1-hour window.

You finished the first week The fleet keeps running between your visits. Next week, start from the markets you already know and widen one hop at a time.

Doctrine reference

Doctrine is the set of rules the fleet follows. Each rule has an on/off switch and, for some rules, a value. The table lists the rules a player is most likely to change. Defaults are the values the fleet starts with.

Margin floor10 cIgnore a route whose profit per unit is below this.
Max lots per trip6How many of a market's trade-volume lots one trip may buy. Higher means bigger trips and more price impact.

Circuit reach10 minHow far apart the two legs of a circuit may be, in flying minutes.

Follow-on lookaheadoff, 50 %Credit a route for the next good trip that can start where it sells.

Worked examples

Example 1

Why is a good route not being flown?

Open the dispatch explanation. Each idle trader lists the reason it was refused each route. Read the reason, not the total.

  • "The first trip does not cover the 3-jump positioning cost." The route is real but too far for its profit. Either leave it, or raise Positioning jumps for a short while.
  • "No finished gate path from X to Y within the jump limit." The gates along the way are not all finished. Wait for construction, or work a nearer cluster first.
  • "Already claimed this cycle." Another ship has it. Nothing to do.

Example 2

Changing a doctrine value from your phone

Tower, then More, then Rules. Find Positioning jumps. Tap the value box, type 4, and leave the box. The new value takes effect on the next dispatch cycle, about a minute later. The Rules list shows the saved number.

Example 3

Checking this week's score

Metrics, then tap Since reset. Read Net trading and Net per hour. Compare the top ships in the table. If you want the wallet, read Wallet change and check cargo still held before you draw a conclusion from it.

Example 4

A ship at the wrong place

A ship sits far from its market with no route. Check the ship sheet: its role, its fuel, and whether it is mid-jump. A ship that is still flying is not stuck. If it has been idle for an hour with fuel and no route, ask the co-pilot for the reason before changing its role.

Common mistakes

Each of these looked like a fault at first and was normal behaviour.

  1. Re-sending a command to a ship already moving. It wastes the order and can interrupt a good flight.
  2. Moving traders by hand. The dispatcher picks the best route for each idle trader. A manual move usually leaves a better trader idle.
  3. Selling a lot into one market at once. Each sale lowers the price there for a while. Spread sales over markets.
  4. Buying faster than a market recovers. Price rises with each purchase. A tight loop can push the price up 8 to 10 percent per load.
  5. Expanding far before the near markets are used. A ship sent across the galaxy spends its first trip on jump fees. Build out one hop at a time from where your markets already are.
  6. Buying probes before traders. Traders make the money. A probe is for pricing a market you cannot see.
  7. Pausing a contract. Contracts have fixed payouts and deadlines. Pausing one can lose the payout.
  8. Routing through an unfinished gate. A gate works only when its construction is complete at both ends. Greyed rows in the jump planner mean the gate is not ready.

Glossary

WordMeaning
ResetThe weekly restart of the universe. Everything is regenerated.
GateThe link between two systems. Finished on both ends before a ship can jump.
PositioningThe cost of moving an idle ship to where a route starts, in jumps and flight time.
CircuitA two-leg trip where the second leg starts near where the first one sells.
DoctrineThe fleet's standing rules: on/off switches and values.
KeeperA ship that stays at one market so its prices stay fresh.
FeedA crew that sources a good and sells it into a market.
ApprovalA purchase the fleet wants to make on its own. You approve or reject it.
ProposalA change the co-pilot queues for you. Nothing happens until you confirm.
